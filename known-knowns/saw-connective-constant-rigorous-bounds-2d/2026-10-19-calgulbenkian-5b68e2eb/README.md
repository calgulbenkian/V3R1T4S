# Connective constant of the 4.8^2 lattice: an exact-rational upper bound 849051343/464433440 = 1.8281442934 (partial win p1)

## What

p1 only: a certified exact-rational upper bound mu(4.8^2) <= 849051343/464433440 = 1.8281442934 (loop length 30, 2,750,336 states), below Alm's 1.829254 (Jensen 2004 Table 3's 1.82926) by about 1.11e-3, with a documented search of the indexed record finding no later bound, though absence in unindexed or paywalled sources cannot be excluded.

The object is the state list and a positive integer test vector of a finite-memory loop-avoiding-walk automaton on the square-octagon (4.8^2) lattice. The checker rebuilds all 4,420,718 transitions from the definition in object/AUTOMATON.md, verifies the subset-inclusion condition of object/THEOREM.md on every one, and computes max (Px)_i / x_i exactly as a fraction.

## Check it yourself

From this package folder, run:

```sh
sh verify-it-yourself/run_checks.sh
```

- Requirements: python3 3.8 or later, standard library only; about 770 MB of memory (the script says up to 1.5 GB to be safe) and about 45 MB of temporary disk.
- Time: about 7 to 15 minutes on a laptop (6 minutes for the full check on an idle Apple-silicon Mac; 11 to 13 minutes when the machine was busy).
- Expected last line: ALL CHECKS PASS (the line before it is PASS, and above that: exact max (P x)_i / x_i = 6792410744 / 3715467520 = 849051343/464433440 = 1.828144293400).

The object is kept as 42 binary parts (37,215,863 bytes in all, each part at most 900,000 bytes) listed with their sha256 in object/SHA256_PARTS.txt; the script reassembles them and checks the sha256 of the whole (5b68e2eb7517096965e583cc68cddb13978e8959b8a0a7a218d08ad10a2dfee7) and of every part before the full check.

## Not claimed

- Only the 4.8^2 lattice and only loop length 30. No claim for the square lattice (the main criterion of the target), the triangular or kagome lattices, or any other loop length. The same team also reported a loop length 32 bound (1.826967) and a loop length 34 pocket-pruned bound (1.825523); no object is shipped for those and they are not claimed.
- This is the partial win p1 only, not the target's main criterion.
- No claim that every loop up to length 30 is excluded by the automaton. The bound rests only on the subset-inclusion condition (SI) proved in object/THEOREM.md, for locally finite vertex-transitive graphs.
- The search for a later, better bound covered the indexed record only (Crossref, OpenAlex cited-by lists, arXiv, and the full texts of Jensen 2004, the weighted-SAW paper arXiv 2508.01993, Alm 2005 and a thesis summary). Google Scholar, MathSciNet, zbMATH, Scopus and Web of Science were not searched; Semantic Scholar returned HTTP 429 and was not retried. Absence of a better bound in unindexed or paywalled sources cannot be excluded.
- The Alm 2005 text read is the manuscript copy from the Internet Archive, not the typeset journal page. Parviainen's thesis was read only as an 11-page summary from an archive copy.
- The certificate (states and integer test vector) was regenerated by our triage from the author's generator (how-we-built-it/saw_bounds_2d.c, loop length 30, option use_bridges = 1), because our snapshot of the author's folder skipped files over 100 MB. Two regenerations by our agents agree byte for byte; the author's own copy was not compared. The checker does not trust the generator: it rebuilds the transitions from the definition.
- Four fixes were made after the reproduce and novelty skeptics ran, following a read-only review by a model of a different family (OpenAI gpt-6.1-sol, clean with fixes): the theorem setting was restricted to vertex-transitive graphs, no claim is made that every loop up to length 30 is excluded, and the checker now requires the exact ratio, K = 30 and 2,750,336 states and checks canonical form. The object is unchanged; the re-run passed (verify-it-yourself/RECEIPTS.md).
- The three panel skeptics are Claude Code subagents; the object's builder (Gemini, Argon) is a different family from every skeptic. Not peer reviewed. The checker's lattice and symmetry checks run on a finite window and rely on period 3 of the lattice.
- The choice of loop length 30 is post hoc (the smallest length whose bound passes the target); the claim is one exact certified number.
